Pitch: 5 minutes (about 2 min of slides, then live demo)

Your keys. Your control.

A self-custody wallet one step from your Swissquote account. The bank builds the tool, but never holds the keys.

The demonstration: A + B.
A: the client's own key, always able to spend alone.
B: a rule enforced by the chain, not by Swissquote: Swissquote and a trusted contact recover together, only after a delay.
From A + B, four properties follow, each tested on a real node: Swissquote alone can do nothing; with the contact, before the delay, the network refuses; a client who stays active restarts the delay; a lost key is recoverable after the delay.

Likely questions

A Swissquote employee with bad intentions tries to open my crypto area from their own PC? Their biometrics unlock their key, not mine. The server checks a signature against the public key registered for my account, so it refuses. Two limits, said plainly: the first passkey is registered with the session alone, so enrol it right after creating the account; and the "I lost my device" path gives a limited level after a re-KYC (mocked here: name, birth date, code), where production needs real identity checks. And an insider who controls the bank server can bypass the lock, because it is a server rule. They still cannot move funds from my wallet: the key is on my device behind its own biometrics, and a recovery needs the contact and the delay.
You said you looked at MPC and a smart wallet: why not just use them? MPC cannot enforce a delay on-chain: Swissquote and a partner can sign immediately, and the delay is a rule in software they run. A smart wallet exists only on Ethereum, so it cannot cover Bitcoin. We use one (a Safe) on Ethereum. The security gain of MPC against a stolen device is real, and we say so: a stolen seed lets a thief spend at once in our design.
What if Swissquote disappears? On Bitcoin, the Emergency exit in the Client Wallet reads the coins from the chain and signs with the client's key alone, without the site or the bank server. It needs the device with its wallet. If the client loses the device and Swissquote is gone, the funds are lost (stated in the README). Ethereum has no exit kit yet.
Can the client change their trusted contact? Yes on Bitcoin: it changes the script, so the coins move to a new address and the delay restarts.
Is Swissquote custodian? Alone, it can move nothing. With the contact, it can after the delay, to any destination (the grey zone). The client resets the delay by spending.
Does it make money? Not on fees alone at our illustrative values (break-even about 2,730 retained clients). The case rests on Swissquote's real volumes and on the strategic value of keeping the client.
Is the gasless real? Real, through Candide's public paymaster (a third party). No abuse control of our own.
Compliant dApp? Not implemented. The wallet decodes operations in plain language and refuses what it cannot explain.
Why does the repository differ from the demo? The submission is the state pushed at 09:48 and 09:49. After 10:00, we added the key-pasting fix and a demo client, and merged the biometric-lock branch locally. Nothing was pushed or backdated.

To check before presenting

  • The validation reports of the biometric lock, the Emergency exit, the device-bound wallet and the contact change come from another session and were not re-run. Only the "crypto locked" refusal was observed directly.
  • The MPC library limits were read in its documentation, not executed. The smart-wallet problems come from risks identified in the spec: do not say "it failed".
  • All business-case figures are illustrative, none comes from Swissquote. The "no custody burden" point is a hypothesis to confirm with a lawyer.
